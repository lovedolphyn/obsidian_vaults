The **corridor** beyond the door continues **about 20 feet** (6 m) before opening into quite a large chamber that contains the **mechanism** called the [[Taker of Sorrow]] (==the floating metallic hoop==).

<mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">This massive chamber is a hollow in the red metal and lined with streaks of glowing white that spread like veins across the floor, rounded walls, and ceiling. They all lead to a mechanism fused with the far wall that features a floating metallic hoop, suspended upright just a few inches above the floor. A man who wears a form-fitting metallic helmet and elaborate robes in every shade of green is also present, leaning over a central cavity that might well act as a drain if the entire room ever filled with water.</mark>

##### If the PCs Waited Until They Were Sent for: 
[[Weymel]] is just cleaning the last of some blood left behind when he used his [[songbirds]] on the most recent failed [[Taker of Sorrow]] supplicant. The supplicant, who disassembled into the latest [[teratoma]] pack, fled down the drain, which leads to a chute that drops those who fall in it to Enigram Mountain’s exterior. (When the [[songbirds]] are used in close proximity to teratomas, the creatures sometimes bleed from various orifices as they flee.) 

>**[[Divine Nex]]:** <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">If [[Divine Nex]] is missing</mark>, the supplicant might very well have been the performer! (The PCs might be able to capture the pack later and try to use the [[Taker of Sorrow]] to return the aerosaltant to health, as long as they don’t destroy it.) Otherwise, the victim was just the latest citizen of [[Gram]] to be taken by the Reaper of Sin.

[[Weymel]] **answers questions**, the very soul of cordial hospitality and ascetic calm. Of course, he lies through his teeth to support the **story he’s created to hide the true** situation in [[Gram]]. For instance, if asked about the teratomas or babble mites, <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">he indicates that yes, the town was once troubled by the pests, but they haven’t been a problem in years because of the [[songbirds]].</mark>

[[Weymel]] offers to **schedule** the PCs (<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">or [[Divine Nex]], if the aerosaltant hasn’t already had time with the [[Taker of Sorrow]]</mark>). Odds are high that if a PC uses the machine, it doesn’t turn the character into a [[teratoma]] pack, but instead **boosts their spirits** by draining away old cares. However, that PC **might also become a new ally** for [[Weymel]].

##### If the PCs Forced the Door: 
PCs who arrive in the chamber find [[Weymel]] **leaning over the drain, jabbing the hands of someone** who has been forced into it but is just managing to hang onto the edge.<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;"> If [[Divine Nex]] preceded the characters and has gone missing, that’s who the victim is</mark>; otherwise it’s a random citizen.

Either way, the PCs have only a **few moments** to take it in before they are subjected to a <mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">GM group intrusion</mark>, as the victim disintegrates into jabbering, tumorous lumps, **becoming a [[teratoma]] pack in front of their eyes**.

The [[teratoma]] **pack goes after [[Weymel]]**, who’s right there, but the serein **has his [[songbirds]] ready**. He uses them to **drive the pack down the drain**. PCs who witness this can form their own conclusions, but [[Weymel]] tries to draw on the newcomers’ **sympathy** by yelling <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">“Help me!”</mark> If the PCs comply, they don’t need to do much because the [[songbirds]] do the job well. However, [[Weymel]] makes a **big show of gratitude**.

If the PCs mention that they just **saw a person turn into a [[teratoma]] pack**, he knows that no lie is likely to convince them to doubt the evidence of their own eyes. <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">“You saw that, did you? I’m afraid that, very rarely, the [[Taker of Sorrow]] . . . malfunctions.”</mark> With that, [[Weymel]] makes a panicked **attack** on them.

Each time [[Weymel]] **attacks** a foe, the [[Taker of Sorrow]] responds with a **preprogrammed blast**—a **long-range psychic ray** directed at another creature in the chamber. On a failed ==Intellect defense roll==, the target takes **6 points of Intellect damage** *and* **loses their next turn**.

##### If the PCs and [[Weymel]] Fight: 
>If the characters see through [[Weymel]]’s act and **attack him, or** if **he** panics and **attacks first**, it’s likely that he’ll lose. He’s no idiot, so ==after a round or two of combat==, he screams <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">“Wait! If you kill me, you kill everyone who the Reaper of Sin stole away—or, as it actually happened, disintegrated into a pack of teratomas. Only I can bring them back. If you kill me, you consign all of them to eternal existence as mindless horrors. Swear on your honor to spare me, and I will save them!”</mark> 

> [!faq] No Deal!
> If the PCs **don’t want a deal**, [[Weymel]] responds in kind and **fights to the end**.

> [!faq] Deal?
> If the PCs are true to their word, or seem to be, **[[Weymel]] helps them** begin the process of **reassembling** [[teratoma]] packs using a **modified** [[Taker of Sorrow]]. As it turns out, the songbird devices can be tuned to **lure teratomas** as well as expel them, and the [[Taker of Sorrow]] can be further modified to **reverse the process** of disintegration.
