# Adventure Overview
*Source: Numenera Discovery, p. 363* 
![[Img - Taker of Sorrow Banner.jpg]]
 
**Taker of Sorrow** is meant to be a GM’s first Numenera adventure. It is presented with as much advice and assistance as a new GM will need. It even comes with a flowchart that shows the course of the adventure. The flowchart is an example of how GMs might write up their own adventures—a few sentences are all you really need.

This adventure is great for beginning characters. Given the inclusion of several optional side encounters, you could even begin a new campaign with “Taker of Sorrow.” It is presented in a style that gives new GMs (or at least GMs new to Numenera) helpful hints along the way about how to run the adventure. The beginning is designed so that the PCs have a reason to join together even if their backgrounds and character types are very different.

## SYNOPSIS
A kind of ==contract== known as “**shin obligation**” finds the PCs traveling with a philosopher, athletic performer, and teacher called **[[Divine Nex]]** who travels between several communities in [[Navarene]]. During the journey, side encounters are possible. Partway through the trip, the PCs’ camp attracts a couple of farmers fleeing from a [[teratoma]] pack who plead for assistance ([[E1 - Fleeing the Pack]]).

If the PCs want to help, they can do so directly by backtracking and clearing out a [[teratoma]] nest or indirectly by inviting the farmers to stay with them for safety. 

The farmers live in a nearby village called **[[Vesburgh]]**, which is where [[Divine Nex]] wants to go anyway. [[Vesburgh]] has lately come under a veritable siege from several [[teratoma]] packs. Though the PCs can probably offer short-term aid against the teratomas, characters who are determined to provide a long-term solution can track the creatures to their source, which leads them out of [[Vesburgh]] and to a different town called [[Gram]].

> [!tip] GM Tip
 If the PCs seem disinclined to go to [[Gram]] or become stuck in [[Vesburgh]] because they think they can find a solution there, [[Divine Nex]]—to whom they owe their shin obligation—insists that the group travel to [[Gram]].

In [[Gram]], the characters eventually ==learn its **secret**==: an ascetic named [[Weymel]] seized control of the community using a sacred “spiritual” device known as the [[Taker of Sorrow]], which makes subjects pliable to his will or transforms them into horrific beasts. However, by this time, a PC or perhaps [[Divine Nex]] may have already been subjected to the [[Taker of Sorrow]] themselves, which leaves them either an ally of [[Weymel]] or (if they resist the influence) possibly a new [[teratoma]] pack.

The PCs must decide whether to try to fix the [[Taker of Sorrow]] (the spiritual heart of [[Gram]], and likely the only way to convert a [[teratoma]] pack back into the person it once was) or destroy it. Either way, there are consequences for the choice they make.

## GETTING THE PLAYERS INVOLVED

> [!NOTE] The Hook
> The player characters become involved, and some may even meet each other, because **they all pledged to the same shin obligation**: to protect the philosopher and aerosaltant known as [[Divine Nex]] on their tour of several outlying Navarene communities.

You can use this adventure as the scenario that starts a new campaign. If the PCs begin as strangers, give the players the background about shin obligation ahead of time, along with the fact that they’ve made the pledge. They’ll have to integrate their characters into the adventure by deciding why they pledged.

*Reasons to Pledge [[3-Shin Obligations|Shin Obligation]] Include the
Following:*
- The PC just wants to **do some good**. 
- It’s a **coming-of-age activity** where they grew up, something many people do as a point of honor.
- A **tragedy** in the PC’s life made them want to find something new. 
- It was either pledge to [[3-Shin Obligations|shin obligation]] or go to prison **for a crime** they were charged with (whether or not the PC actually committed the crime is another matter).

*Other Reasons to Get Involved:*
- One character is **friends** with [[Divine Nex]] and wants to make sure the aerosaltant doesn’t come to harm. 
- One of the players decides to play the character of **[[Divine Nex]] (or a similar character)**, in which case they likely possess the [[Entertains focus]]. The knowledge ascribed to [[Divine Nex]] in this adventure is instead available to the PC who takes on this role.

## ADVENTURE TIMELINE
If you like, use the following timeline to help you organize the adventure when you run it for your players. 

> [!tip] GM Tip for Handling Days: 
> Though the characters in the game experience each hour of each day, you and your players should not. Only handle the exciting or informational parts of each day. Then, if you know that nothing else is likely to happen for the rest of the day or night, just tell your players that another day has passed, so they break camp and travel for several hours, at which point something new or interesting happens (which you describe). You’re not obligated to sketch out each day—just the exciting parts, as well as some opportunities that allow the PCs to interact with each other.

> [!tip] GM Tip for Downtime Scenes: 
> Sometimes, “downtime” scenes where player characters interact with each other are just as important and fun for the game as exciting scenes. Here are several ways to facilitate downtime scenes: As the characters are setting up camp one night after a day of travel and adventure, ask the PCs what they’re doing; ask them to share a story or answer a question from [[Divine Nex]] about how they became an explorer; use a **GM intrusion** to bring to light a relationship between two PCs that was not previously known; and so on.

>**Day 1** ==Tell the PCs their situation==: They’re traveling with [[Divine Nex]] as part of their shin obligation. Ask that each PC introduce themselves to the others. Describe [[Divine Nex]] as you interpret the performer.

> **Day 2 (Optional)**: ==Run one of the [[3-Shin Obligations|Shin Obligations]] encounters==—maybe **[[SO1 - Taking A Toll|Taking a Toll]]**.

> **Day 3** ==Run Encounter 1==: **[[E1 - Fleeing the Pack|Fleeing the Pack]]**, where the PCs meet farmers fleeing a [[teratoma]] pack. 

> **Day 4** If the PCs choose to follow the farmers back to their farm, ==run Encounter 2==: **[[E2 - Farm Nest|Farm Nest]]**. Otherwise (or afterward), the PCs probably head toward [[Vesburgh]].

> **Day 5** The PCs probably ==head to [[Vesburgh]]== (**[[E3 - Vesburgh|Encounter 3]]**), where they likely spend some time. Here, they’re asked to help with the plague of [[Teratoma Packs (Babble Mites)|teratomas]] afflicting the community.

> **Day 6** The PCs ==head out and travel toward== either the town of [[Gram]] **or** (far less likely) the [[2. Vault of Reflections|Vault of Reflections]].

> **Day 7 (Optional)**: ==Run one of the [[3-Shin Obligations|Shin Obligation]] encounters==, potentially **[[SO2 - Ravage No More|Ravage No More]]**.

> **Day 8+** The PCs arrive at the site where they believe the [[Teratoma Packs (Babble Mites|teratomas]] originate. If that’s [[Gram]], ==run Encounter 5==: **[[E5 - Gram|Gram]]**. (If it’s the **[[E4 - Vault of Reflections|Vault of Reflections]]**, refer to Chapter 26). Assuming the PCs are in [[Gram]], they’ll spend **one or more days here**, following up on leads described in Encounter 5, ==Encounter 6==: **[[E6 - Supplicant Hostel|Supplicant Hostel]]**, and the ==adventure finale in Encounter 7==: **[[E7 - Chamber of Sorrow|Chamber of Sorrow]]**. Depending on how the PCs deal with the situation, they could end up spending **several more days** attempting to lure surviving teratomas back to be reassembled, **or** just **destroy the mechanism** they find to end the adventure. 

>[!tip] GM Tip for Wrapping Things Up: 
>You can play out how the PCs spend a few days attempting to lure surviving teratomas back to be reassembled in part or in whole, or you can just describe how well they succeed. However, many groups will simply destroy the [[Taker of Sorrow]] device that creates teratomas and call it a success, which is a fine way to conclude the adventure as well.

## SHIN OBLIGATION ENCOUNTERS
During this adventure, the PCs spend much of their time traveling. The characters can use the time to deepen their relationships with one another, ==describing their purpose for taking on the **shin obligation pledge**, and recounting and elaborating upon connections they may have with other PCs==. 

The group will **need to camp** after each day’s travel during a journey that lasts at least a week, if not more. (*PCs have likely already spent a few days on the road even before the first day noted on the adventure timeline begins.*) 

>[!tip] GM Tip
>GMs can add **side encounters** to the journey, if desired. These encounters can be introduced anywhere and at any time while the PCs are not staying in a village or town like [[Vesburgh]] or [[Gram]].
